Warning signs

How to tell if your turbo is failing.

Loss of power

A noticeable drop in acceleration, particularly at higher revs or under load, is one of the first signs of turbo failure. The engine may feel flat or sluggish where it previously felt responsive. This is caused by the turbo failing to generate sufficient boost pressure.

Blue or black smoke from the exhaust

Blue smoke indicates oil is being burned — often caused by worn turbo seals allowing oil into the intake. Black smoke indicates over-fuelling, which can be caused by the turbo over-boosting due to a faulty wastegate. Both require immediate attention.

Whining or whistling noise

A high-pitched whine from the engine bay — particularly under acceleration — can indicate worn turbo bearings or a cracked turbo housing. The noise may change pitch as the turbo spools up and down.

Check engine light

Turbo boost pressure faults, wastegate faults and turbo speed sensor faults all trigger the engine management light. A proper diagnostic read — not just a code clear — is essential to identify turbo-related fault codes accurately.

What's included in a turbo replacement?

We replace the turbocharger unit, all associated oil feed and return lines, inlet and outlet connections, and carry out an oil and filter change as standard. Finding and fixing the root cause of the turbo failure — whether oil starvation, over-revving or a blocked oil feed — is essential to prevent the replacement unit failing in the same way.

Pricing

Turbo replacement costs vary by make, model and whether the unit is a standard or variable geometry turbo. We source quality replacement units and provide a full written quote before any work starts. Call or message us with your vehicle details for an accurate estimate.
