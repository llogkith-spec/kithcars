Warning signs

How do you know if your clutch needs replacing?

A worn clutch doesn't usually fail suddenly — it gives warning signs over time. The earlier you catch it, the simpler and cheaper the repair.

Slipping clutch

The most obvious sign — you press the accelerator and the engine revs climb, but the car doesn't accelerate as expected. The clutch friction plate has worn too thin to transfer power effectively. Often noticeable on hills or when pulling away in a higher gear.

High or inconsistent biting point

If the clutch engages very high on the pedal travel — or the biting point has crept up over time — the clutch plate is wearing out. A biting point that varies between journeys can indicate a hydraulic fault in the clutch system.

Difficulty selecting gears

Grinding when changing gear, resistance when selecting a gear, or the gearbox jumping out of gear are all signs the clutch isn't fully disengaging. This can be a clutch plate issue or a problem with the clutch release bearing or fork.

Burning smell

A sharp, acrid burning smell — particularly after pulling away from a junction or hill start — indicates the clutch plate is slipping against the flywheel. This smell is distinctive and should be taken seriously.

Spongy or stiff pedal

A clutch pedal that feels spongy, requires excessive force, or feels different to usual can indicate worn clutch components, a failing release bearing, or a hydraulic fault. All require investigation.

Clutch judder or vibration

A shudder or vibration through the car when pulling away — particularly on a cold engine — can indicate a worn clutch plate, oil contamination on the clutch face, or a failing dual-mass flywheel.

What we do

What's included in a clutch replacement.

Full inspection before quoting

We inspect the clutch system fully before confirming the quote. This includes checking the release bearing, fork, master and slave cylinders, and the flywheel condition — so there are no surprises mid-job.

Full clutch kit — not just the friction plate

We replace the complete clutch kit: friction plate, pressure plate and release bearing. Replacing only the worn plate and leaving an ageing pressure plate or bearing is poor practice — it won't last, and the labour cost to revisit the job is the same.

Flywheel inspection and advice

We inspect the flywheel — both solid and dual-mass types — and advise you honestly on its condition. Since the gearbox is already removed, replacing a worn flywheel at the same time saves significant labour cost compared to returning later.

Road test and confirmation

Every clutch replacement is followed by a full road test to confirm correct operation, smooth engagement and no vibration. You'll know exactly how it feels before you drive away.

Important

What is a dual-mass flywheel, and do I need to replace it?

Most modern diesel cars — and many petrol cars — use a dual-mass flywheel (DMF). Unlike a traditional solid flywheel, a DMF uses internal springs and dampeners to smooth out engine vibrations. They work well when new, but they wear over time and often need replacing alongside the clutch.

The problem is that accessing the flywheel requires removing the gearbox — exactly the same labour as a clutch replacement. So if the flywheel is worn and you only replace the clutch, you'll be paying that labour bill twice when the flywheel fails shortly after.

We always inspect the flywheel as part of a clutch job and give you an honest assessment. If it's fine, we'll say so. If it's borderline or worn, we'll explain why it makes economic sense to replace it now rather than later — and let you decide.

Common questions

Clutch replacement FAQs.

How long does a clutch replacement take?

Most clutch replacements take four to six hours — typically a full day in the workshop. Vehicles with dual-mass flywheels or more complex gearbox configurations may take longer. We'll confirm the timeframe with your quote, and a courtesy car is available for longer jobs subject to availability.

How long should a clutch last?

A well-maintained clutch on a modern car should last between 60,000 and 100,000 miles. Town driving, frequent towing, and stop-start commuting accelerate wear. If you drive mainly in London or South London traffic, expecting 60,000–70,000 miles from a clutch is realistic.

Can I still drive with a slipping clutch?

Technically yes in the short term, but every mile driven on a slipping clutch accelerates wear and risks damage to the flywheel. What starts as a clutch plate replacement can become a clutch and flywheel replacement if left too long. Book it in as soon as you notice symptoms.
