Timing Belt & Cambelt Replacement

Your cambelt is one of the most critical components in your engine — and one of the most overlooked. A snapped cambelt causes catastrophic engine damage with no warning. We replace cambelts on all makes and models at our Hackbridge workshop, serving Croydon, Sutton, Mitcham and South London.

What you need to know

The cambelt that snaps costs far more than the one that's replaced.

What is a timing belt?

The timing belt (also called a cambelt) is a rubber toothed belt that synchronises the rotation of the crankshaft and camshaft. It controls the precise opening and closing of your engine's valves in time with the pistons. Without it working correctly, your engine cannot run — and if it snaps, the pistons and valves collide, causing catastrophic internal damage.

When does it need replacing?

Most manufacturers recommend cambelt replacement every 60,000 to 80,000 miles or every 4 to 5 years — whichever comes first. Age matters as much as mileage; a rubber belt deteriorates over time regardless of use. Check your vehicle handbook or ask us — we'll look it up for your specific make and model.

What's included in the replacement?

We replace the full cambelt kit — belt, tensioner and idler pulleys. The water pump is also typically replaced at the same time since it's accessed during the same job. Doing both together saves significant labour cost compared to doing them separately.

What does it cost?

Cambelt replacement typically costs between £300 and £700 depending on your make and model. Some engines with complex layouts cost more due to additional labour time. We provide a full written quote before any work starts — no surprises.

Can I still drive with an overdue cambelt?

Technically yes — until it snaps. There is no warning when a cambelt fails. One moment the car is running, the next the engine is destroyed. If your cambelt is overdue by mileage or age, book it in immediately. The replacement cost is a fraction of an engine rebuild.

Is it different from a timing chain?

Yes. A timing chain is metal and designed to last the life of the engine (though it can still wear — see our timing chain page). A timing belt is rubber and must be replaced at set intervals. Check your handbook — your engine has one or the other, not both.
