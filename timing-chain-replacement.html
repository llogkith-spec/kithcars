Warning signs

How do you know if your timing chain needs replacing?

The timing chain keeps your engine's valves and pistons perfectly synchronised. When it wears, the symptoms are distinctive — and the sooner you act, the less damage it causes.

Rattling or clattering on startup

A metallic rattle from the engine when you first start the car — often disappearing once warmed up — is the most common sign of a stretched or worn timing chain. This noise comes from the chain slapping against its guide or tensioner.

Check engine light

A worn timing chain causes the engine's camshaft and crankshaft to fall out of sync. The ECU detects this as a timing fault and illuminates the check engine light, often alongside a misfiring engine.

Rough idle or misfires

As the chain stretches, valve timing becomes inaccurate. This causes rough idling, hesitation under acceleration, and misfires — particularly noticeable in cold weather when oil pressure is lower.

Difficulty starting

In more severe cases, a badly worn or jumped chain can cause difficulty starting the engine at all. At this stage, there is real risk of engine damage if the vehicle is driven further.

Poor fuel economy

Incorrect valve timing caused by a worn chain reduces engine efficiency. A noticeable drop in fuel economy — particularly alongside other symptoms — is worth investigating.

Oil contamination warning

Timing chain tensioners rely on oil pressure to function. Low oil levels or contaminated oil accelerate wear. If you're also seeing oil pressure warnings, a timing chain check is essential.

What we do

What's included in a timing chain replacement.

Full diagnostic first

Before any work starts we connect to the vehicle's ECU and carry out a timing chain-specific assessment to confirm the fault and assess the extent of wear. We won't just take your word for it or assume — we verify.

Timing chain kit — not just the chain

A proper timing chain job replaces the full kit: chain, tensioner, guides and sprockets. Replacing only the chain and leaving worn guides or a failing tensioner is a false economy that leads to repeat failure. We always replace the full kit.

Oil and filter change

Fresh oil and a new filter are included as standard. A worn timing chain is often aggravated by contaminated oil — fitting a new chain into old oil defeats the purpose of the job.

Road test and re-check

Once the chain is fitted and the engine reassembled, we carry out a full road test and re-scan the ECU to confirm no fault codes remain. You get a clear bill of health before collection.

Common questions

Timing chain FAQs.

Can I still drive with a worn timing chain?

We strongly advise against it. A worn chain can snap without warning, causing catastrophic engine damage — bent valves, damaged pistons, a written-off engine. If you suspect timing chain issues, stop driving and get it checked immediately. The repair cost is far lower than an engine replacement.

Is a timing chain the same as a timing belt?

No. A timing belt (also called a cambelt) is a rubber belt that requires replacement at set mileage intervals — typically every 60,000–80,000 miles. A timing chain is metal and is designed to last the life of the engine — but can still wear, particularly on certain engines that are known to be affected.

How long does the job take?

For most common vehicles the job takes one to two full days depending on the engine layout and the extent of disassembly required. We'll give you a realistic timeframe with your quote. A courtesy car is available for longer jobs — subject to availability.
