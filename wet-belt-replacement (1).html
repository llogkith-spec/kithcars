Specialist knowledge required

Wet belt systems need a specialist — not a general garage.

The wrong diagnosis or an incomplete repair on a wet belt engine leads to the same outcome: catastrophic engine damage.

What is a wet belt?

A wet belt (TBIO — timing belt in oil) runs inside the engine immersed in oil rather than in a dry housing. Designed to reduce friction, but requires clean, regular oil changes to function correctly.

Which engines are affected?

Ford 1.0 EcoBoost (Fiesta, Focus, EcoSport), Renault and Nissan engines sharing the same architecture, and several other modern small-capacity engines.

What goes wrong?

Contaminated or degraded oil abrades the belt, causing rapid deterioration. The belt can stretch, skip a tooth or snap — causing the same catastrophic valve and piston damage as a failed cambelt.

Warning signs

Rattling or ticking on startup, rough running, misfires and a check engine light. Visual inspection is impossible without disassembly — diagnosis requires opening the engine.

What's included?

Full wet belt kit — belt, tensioner, idler and seals. Fresh oil and filter change is mandatory. Oil pump and associated components inspected. All work to manufacturer specification.

Prevention

The single most important thing: change the oil on time — or early. Extended oil change intervals are the primary cause of wet belt failure.
