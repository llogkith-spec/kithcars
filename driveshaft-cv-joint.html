Warning signs

CV joint and driveshaft problems — what to look for.

CV joint faults are easy to diagnose if you know what to listen for.

Clicking on full lock

A clicking or clunking noise when turning on full lock — particularly when pulling away — is the classic sign of a worn outer CV joint. The noise is usually absent when driving straight.

Vibration when accelerating

A vibration felt through the car when accelerating, particularly at higher speeds, can indicate a worn inner CV joint or a damaged driveshaft.

Torn CV boot

CV joints are protected by a rubber boot filled with grease. If the boot splits, the grease escapes and contaminants enter — the joint fails rapidly once unprotected. Replacing a torn boot early prevents the need to replace the whole joint.

CV boot replacement

Where the CV joint itself is still serviceable but the boot is torn, we replace the boot and repack with fresh grease — avoiding the cost of a full joint replacement.

CV joint replacement

Where the joint is worn beyond recovery, we replace the outer or inner CV joint as required. We confirm inner or outer, left or right before ordering parts.

Full driveshaft replacement

In some cases a complete driveshaft replacement is more cost-effective than individual joint replacement. We advise honestly on which is the better option for your vehicle.
